The action: what you send back

Two numbers in [-1, 1]. Both are relative to what you are doing now, and the command is held for one decision interval (10 simulated seconds).

The dashed purple arrow on both maps is the heading you would command. The aircraft does not jump to it: it turns toward it over the next several seconds. Speed is already part of the action space. Our policy-gradient testbed sends np.array([a, 0.0]), so it never changes speed.

Things that are easy to get wrong

  • Two sign conventions. Goal, obstacle and sector bearings use heading − bearing, so a positive sin means the target is on your left. Intruder y_r is positive on your right. And a positive heading action turns right. (Our testbed uses “positive = right” everywhere.)
  • cos_track / sin_track is not the intruder's compass heading. It is the direction of the relative velocity (intruder minus you), measured from your nose. Look at the arrow in the right-hand view.
  • Slots are re-sorted by distance every step. Slot 0 is whoever is closest right now, so the same aircraft moves between slots. Nearest is not the same as most dangerous: pick the intruder in the “intruders” tab and check its closest-approach box.
  • Closest approach (TCPA, DCPA) is not in the observation. The code can use it as a sorting key, but by default the sort is by distance, and the numbers themselves are never handed over. The network has to infer them from position and relative velocity.
  • Obstacles are a centroid plus an enclosing radius. The true shape is a polygon (scored exactly), so the dashed circle is often much bigger than the red shape.
  • No absolute position or heading appears anywhere. Your own heading only enters through the drift angle to the goal. Other aircraft's speed only enters through relative velocity.
  • Padding looks like an object at distance 0. Unused slots are all zeros, which is also what an object exactly on top of you would give.

Where the numbers come from

Formulas, units and normalisers are ported from core/observations.py and core/actions.py, with the settings CompetitionEnv uses (distance_norm=170 km, intruder positions in metres ÷ 106, velocities ÷ 150 m/s, intruder distance NM ÷ 250, obstacle radius ÷ 17.84 NM, airspeed (m/s − 150) ÷ 20). The scene itself is made up: real scenarios have 5 obstacles, 10 intruders on routes, a sector of 15,000–23,000 NM² and a start-to-goal distance of 100–170 km. The observation code on this page was checked against the real CompetitionEnv on 30 states from 6 seeds (3,930 values compared, largest difference 2×10−15).
